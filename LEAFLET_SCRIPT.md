# Leaflet script

**Team:** Rapid Builders
**What it is:** a folded card, made from one A4 sheet folded in half.
**Its job:** the panel will forget slides. The card is what stays on the table after we leave.

---

## What is on the card

**Front cover: one customer, quietly leaving.**
The title, "Customer Lifetime Value", sits under a piece of artwork: one year in the life of customer C10000, drawn as twelve rings of dots. Every dot is a day. The black dots are the days she came to shop. The orange in the middle is the long silence since her last visit, 102 days. Nobody noticed she'd gone quiet.

**Inside: two customers, two decisions.**
- **Left page, C10000:** a loyal family shopper. She's probably slipping away, and she's worth a lot to us, about £345 a year. So we invest in her: early access and an offer on the clothes she likes.
- **Right page, C10001:** an occasional shopper with small baskets. She's slipping away too, but she's worth much less, about £33 a year. So we spend very little: one automated email.
- **Across the middle:** "Not every customer deserves the same pound."

**Back cover: what's in it for Primark.**
- For every £1 spent on these targeted campaigns, about £4 comes back. That's roughly £3 kept after paying for the campaign.
- The coloured squares show where attention goes: a small group of valuable customers gets personal care, and most customers get something cheap or nothing extra.
- For every 1,000 customers Primark can identify, that's roughly £3,000 a year kept.

## What to say while handing over

One line, said quietly, the same to everyone:

> **"Her year, one dot per day. Open it for the two customers you just met."**

If someone looks up and engages, add one more line at most:

> "The back is what it means for Primark."

Then move on. The whole handout should take under 40 seconds.

1. **Cover:** "This is one customer's year. Every dot is a day; the black ones are her visits. The orange is the silence since her last one. Nobody noticed."
2. **Open it:** "Two customers, both drifting away. One is worth a lot, so we invest. One is worth little, so we spend almost nothing."
3. **Turn it over:** "Across everyone, the money goes where it pays back: about £3 kept for every £1 spent."

## Likely questions and answers

Answer in one or two sentences, then stop.

**What is the artwork on the cover?**
One customer's year. Every dot is a day, the black dots are her shopping visits, and the orange in the middle is the long silence since she last came in. That silence is the warning sign our system picks up.

**Are these real customers?**
No. Everything is made-up data built to behave like real shoppers, and the card says so. These two are examples of the two ends of the decision.

**Why spend so little on the second customer if she's even more likely to leave?**
Because she's worth very little to us. Spending a lot to win her back would cost more than she'd bring in. The spend follows the value.

**What does "£1 → £4" mean?**
For every pound we put into these targeted campaigns, about four pounds of sales come back. After paying for the campaign, we keep about three.

**Isn't that too good to be true?**
It's based on deliberately cautious assumptions: that only a small share of customers respond. Even if we're wrong by half, it still pays. A small test on real customers would give the true figure.

**Why do you only win back part of the money at risk?**
Because most customers who drift away won't come back whatever you do. We don't chase all of them; we chase the ones worth chasing.

**What are the coloured squares?**
Each square is one in every hundred customers. A small group gets personal attention, some are rewarded for loyalty, half get one cheap message, and the rest are simply left alone.

**What does "per 1,000 customers" mean for Primark?**
It's what our example data shows for every thousand customers Primark can identify: roughly £3,000 kept a year. The more customers Primark knows through the app and its systems, the bigger that gets. A pilot on real data would give the real number.

**How do we know the numbers are right?**
We tested the system on a full year it had never seen, and it predicted what customers actually spent more accurately than the standard method retailers use today. The real proof would be a pilot on Primark's own data.

**Can we see the screens again?**
Yes. We can send the slides, or show the live tool after the session.

## Before printing: checklist

1. **Numbers match the screen.** These come from the run on 13 Oct, with the Overview sliders at Protect 20% and Win back 12%:
   - **Inside:** C10000 73% and £345; C10001 92% and £33.
   - **Back:** £1 → £4.15, the 9/21/50/20 squares, and £4.2k / £1.0k / £3.2k per 1,000 customers.

   If anyone re-runs the system or moves a slider, check the screens again before printing.
2. **Export.** From the leaflet, export "All artboards (.pdf)": two A4 landscape pages, Outside first, then Inside.
3. **Print.** Double-sided at 100% (actual size), with **flip on short edge**. Use thick card (160 to 200 gsm) if your printer takes it.
4. **Fold.** Fold in half with the ring artwork on the outside, using the small tick marks at the top and bottom edge as the guide. Crease firmly with a ruler.
5. **Proof one copy first.** When you open it, C10000 must be on the left and C10001 on the right, with nothing cut off at the edges.
6. **Quantity.** One per panellist, three spares, and two for rehearsal.
